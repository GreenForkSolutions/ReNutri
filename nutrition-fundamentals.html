Nutrition Fundamentals.

A beginner-friendly guide to the nutrients our bodies use for energy, growth, maintenance, and brain health. Nutrition supports wellbeing, but is not a substitute for professional mental health or addiction care.

Macronutrients & Micronutrients

Macronutrients are needed in relatively large quantities: protein, dietary fat, and carbohydrate. They supply energy and/or materials the body uses to build and maintain tissues. Micronutrients are vitamins and minerals needed in smaller amounts to support many bodily processes.

Protein & Amino Acids

Proteins are built from 20 common amino acids. Nine are essential in the diet because the body cannot make enough of them.

Tryptophan is an essential amino acid used to produce serotonin. Phenylalanine is essential and can be converted into tyrosine, which the body uses to make dopamine. These pathways also depend on enzymes and vitamin and mineral cofactors; eating a particular food does not guarantee an immediate change in mood.

Dietary Fats

Four familiar categories are saturated, monounsaturated, polyunsaturated, and trans fats. Unsaturated fats are generally preferred over saturated fats; industrial trans fats should be avoided.

Polyunsaturated fats include omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids, both essential. Omega-3 fats contribute to normal brain function. Plant sources of ALA omega-3 include hemp, chia, and flax seeds and walnuts. Seafood such as salmon, sardines, and trout supplies EPA and DHA, which the body can use directly. Grass-fed beef and dairy, and eggs from hens fed flaxseed, may contain some omega-3, but amounts vary and are generally much smaller than in oily fish.

Carbohydrates

Simple carbohydrates include sugars, while complex carbohydrates include starches and dietary fibre. Starch contains two glucose polymers: amylose (mostly straight chains) and amylopectin (branched chains). Their proportions and food preparation can affect digestion. Whole grains, beans, vegetables, and fruit also provide fibre and micronutrients.

Vitamins

Vitamin A

Preformed vitamin A includes retinol, retinal, and retinoic acid. Plants provide provitamin A carotenoids such as beta-carotene, alpha-carotene, and beta-cryptoxanthin. Vitamin A supports vision, immunity, and cell development.

B-complex: all eight

  • B1 (thiamin): carbohydrate energy metabolism and nerve function.
  • B2 (riboflavin): energy-producing reactions.
  • B3 (niacin): NAD/NADP coenzymes used in energy metabolism.
  • B5 (pantothenic acid): component of coenzyme A.
  • B6 (pyridoxine): amino-acid metabolism and neurotransmitter synthesis.
  • B7 (biotin): enzymes involved in fat and carbohydrate metabolism.
  • B9 (folate): DNA synthesis and red blood cell formation.
  • B12 (cobalamin): nerve health, DNA synthesis, and red blood cells.

Vitamins C, D, E & K

C: antioxidant activity, collagen formation, and improved non-heme iron absorption. D: calcium absorption, bone health, and normal muscle function. E: fat-soluble antioxidant that helps protect cell membranes; its established role is antioxidant protection rather than treatment of inflammation. K: helps activate proteins required for blood clotting and supports bone metabolism.

Minerals & Electrolytes

  • Calcium: bones, teeth, muscle contraction, and nerve signalling.
  • Phosphorus: bones and teeth, ATP energy transfer, and cell membranes; phosphate also acts as an electrolyte.
  • Iron: oxygen transport in haemoglobin and energy metabolism.
  • Zinc: immune function, wound healing, and many enzymes.
  • Magnesium: enzyme activity, muscle and nerve function, and energy metabolism.
  • Manganese: enzyme function, metabolism, and antioxidant defences.
  • Potassium: fluid balance, nerve signals, and muscle function.
  • Sodium: extracellular fluid balance and nerve and muscle function.
  • Chloride: fluid and acid–base balance and stomach acid production.

Potassium, sodium, chloride, and phosphate are electrolytes; calcium and magnesium also carry electrical charges in body fluids. A varied diet usually supplies these nutrients, though individual needs differ.
